# One Crap Rules: A Wargame You Can Learn in Minutes

One-page rulesets are becoming increasingly popular in the miniature gaming community. Players love the worlds, the terrain, the painted armies, and the spectacle of tabletop warfare—but not everyone wants to spend an evening searching through rulebooks, checking charts, calculating modifiers, and debating special abilities.

**One Crap Rules** takes that idea one step further.

What if the rules weren't merely one page?

What if the actual game could be understood in **a few minutes**, played almost anywhere, and still create those wonderful moments of tension, luck, triumph, disaster, and laughter that make tabletop gaming fun?

And what if we threw in a little **casino flavor** while we were at it?

Welcome to **One Crap Rules**.

## Miniature Wargaming Stripped to the Bones

At its heart, One Crap Rules is a tiny **2d6 miniature wargame**.

You don't need army books. You don't need complicated profiles. You don't need separate values for Strength, Toughness, Armor, Leadership, Ballistic Skill, Weapon Skill, or half a dozen special abilities.

Every model follows essentially the same rules.

A typical army contains only **three to five models**, with four being a great standard game.

Each model has:

* **3" Move**
* **6" Shooting Range**
* **1" Fighting Range**
* **2 Wounds**

That's basically your character sheet.

The battlefield can be as small as **18" × 18" or 24" × 24"**, making the game perfect for a kitchen table, coffee table, desk, or a small piece of terrain board.

The turn sequence is equally simple:

**Priority → Move → Shoot → Fight → End**

If you've played almost any miniature wargame before, you already understand the basic idea.

If you've never played a miniature wargame before, you'll probably understand it after your first turn.

## The Craps Engine

The heart of the game is the familiar roll of **two six-sided dice**.

Instead of consulting multiple attack and defense charts, One Crap Rules uses recognizable craps-style numbers.

When shooting:

**7 or 11?**

Hit.

**2, 3, or 12?**

Something bad happens.

Everything else?

Keep fighting.

Close combat follows the same philosophy. Roll two dice, look at the number, and immediately know what happened.

There is very little mathematical overhead between **rolling the dice** and **seeing the result on the battlefield**.

That is intentional.

The dice aren't supposed to interrupt the game.

**The dice are the game.**

## Rocks, Pebbles, or an Army Worth Fighting For

Technically, you don't even need miniatures.

Grab some rocks.

Use pennies.

Bottle caps work.

Beans probably work.

If you can tell one side from the other, you've got an army.

But let's be serious.

We're miniature gamers.

Half the fun is putting something cool on the battlefield.

Painted fantasy warriors, Space Marines, historical soldiers, cowboys, robots, zombies, little green army men, 3D-printed monsters—whatever you have.

The rules don't care.

That's part of the point.

**One Crap Rules is miniature agnostic.**

Your figures provide the theme.

The rules provide the fight.

You can spend twenty dollars on your army or two hundred. The game plays exactly the same.

And there's something wonderfully satisfying about seeing a beautifully painted warrior standing behind a ruined wall, rolling two ordinary dice, and suddenly hearing:

**“ELEVEN!”**

## Two Wounds. That's It.

Every model gets two wounds.

First wound?

Put a token next to the miniature.

Second wound?

Remove it.

There are no wound charts and no declining stat lines.

A wounded warrior still moves, shoots, and fights normally.

Why?

Because bookkeeping is the enemy.

The goal isn't to simulate the exact medical consequences of being hit by an Orc axe.

The goal is to know:

**Is he still fighting?**

Yes.

Roll the dice.

## Then Comes the Spice

Once everyone understands the core game—and that doesn't take very long—you can turn to page two:

# YO! — A Bit of Spice

This is where the casino starts creeping onto the battlefield.

Players can earn **Battle Chips** from lucky rolls and spend them for small advantages.

A natural **7** can earn a chip.

An **11** can earn even more.

You can spend chips to reroll dice, adjust a result, or squeeze out another action.

But the real fun comes from gambling them.

Wound an enemy?

You can stop.

Or look across the table and say:

**“PRESS!”**

Attack again.

Win and you get rewarded.

Fail and you may lose your chip.

Now we're playing craps.

You can call:

**“YO!”**

Wager a chip before rolling.

Hit exactly **11** and the table erupts.

You can try the **Hard Way**, betting that doubles are coming.

You can become a **High Roller**, putting your accumulated luck back on the table.

And for even more craps flavor, players can establish **The Point**.

Make your Point before the dreaded seven appears and collect your reward.

Seven out?

Better luck next time.

None of this is required.

That's important.

The casino rules are **spice**, not the meal.

Play the core game exactly as written and you've got an extremely fast miniature battle.

Add Battle Chips and suddenly you've got players calling numbers, pressing their luck, celebrating improbable rolls, and wondering whether they should bank their winnings or risk everything.

## Learn in Minutes. Addicted for Life.

That's the philosophy behind One Crap Rules.

The rules should disappear quickly enough that the players can concentrate on the battlefield.

You shouldn't need three games before you understand what you're allowed to do.

You shouldn't need to stop the game every few minutes to check a book.

Move your warrior.

Take the shot.

Charge the enemy.

Roll the dice.

**Seven!**

**Snake Eyes!**

**YO!**

**PRESS!**

Suddenly everyone understands what's happening.

You can teach it in minutes.

You can play with rocks and pebbles if you want.

But you'll probably end up painting miniatures.

Then building terrain.

Then making objective markers.

Then arguing that your tiny plastic warrior absolutely deserved that eleven.

And that's miniature gaming.

**One Crap Rules.**

Small armies.

Small battlefield.

Two dice.

A little strategy.

A little luck.

And just enough casino spice to make you want to roll one more time.
